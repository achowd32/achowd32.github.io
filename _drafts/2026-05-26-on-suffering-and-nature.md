Every reasonable person I've encountered is of the mind that suffering is bad; crucially, most people extend this to non-human suffering as well. Even the most fervent steak-enthusiast will tell you that she would rather an individual cow live a happy life rather than die miserably, all else held equal (i.e. she won't get to eat the cow). We see this propensity to care about animal suffering everywhere in society — people are quick to hurl vile insults at the perpatrator in the latest animal abuse case, and routinely avert their gaze from displays of horrific violence even in the natural world. Perhaps the existence of online spaces like <a href="https://www.reddit.com/r/natureismetal/">r/natureismetal</a> casts doubt on the veracity of my claim, but I maintain that a majority of people are horrified and saddened upon seeing a deer gruesomely mauled alive by a lion, even if a few of them watch on with (what I assume to be) morbid curiosity.

In parallel, I want to illuminate another worldview that lurks in the human spirit: the propensity to romanticize nature. Who among us hasn't been moved by the sheer beauty we see when we journey into the wooded mountains or onto the tropical beaches? Many of my favorite artworks are indeed landscapes; the fame of landscape painters and movements like the Hudson River School throughout history are further evidence of this kind of romanticization. And it makes complete sense that we should find such things beautiful — we evolved out of these very environments and lived among them for millennia. There even seems to be <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8125471/#sec3-ijerph-18-04790">empirical evidence</a> that being in nature is good for you, if any of my fellow Computer Science/Engineering majors had any doubt.

What I find particularly fascinating about these two tendencies — aversion to suffering and attraction to nature — is that they seem to be deeply embedded in most people, and yet they exist in <i>complete conflict with one another</i>! If you've caught on to the obvious point that suffering is everywhere in nature, you might be jumping to point out that one can appreciate parts of nature that are divorced from suffering.

That they can simultaneously exist in so many people is a testament to just how subtly insidious our nature truly is.
